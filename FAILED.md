# What did not work (and why)

Judges weigh "attempts that failed" in the 40% technical criterion. Write each one down when it
happens: what we tried, the number that killed it, and our explanation.

| idea | result | why we think it failed | kept? |
|------|--------|------------------------|-------|
| MTP with 1 draft token (vs 2) | eff 1.77x vs 2.10x, p95 TPOT 105 vs 82 ms, A 0.96 | MTP head accepts ~2.85 tokens/step at 2 drafts; 1 draft halves the speed-up while the verify pass still costs a full forward | no, keep 2 |
| Prefix caching on (vLLM default for this hybrid) | 0% hit rate, KV capacity 588K vs 612K tokens | Mamba/GDN "align" mode stores state snapshots per block; the trace has almost no shared prefixes, so it's pure memory overhead | no, disabled |
| GPU power cap 250 W | grader val S 48.7 -> 0.0: p95 TPOT 51 -> 73 ms, SLO met 57 -> 33/100, J/request 213 -> 225 | with MTP each decode step verifies 3 tokens, so decode is compute-bound, not memory-bound; lower clocks slowed every token and pushed requests past the 50 ms SLO. Energy per *on-time* request rose. Our local sweep missed it because it replayed the same prompts with a warm prefix cache | no, 400 W |
| MTP with 3 draft tokens | J per on-time 954 vs 836 (ref), more tokens generated | acceptance length drops from ~2.2 to ~2.0 under load: the single MTP layer is reused for draft 3 and rarely gets it right, so the extra verify compute is wasted | no, keep 2 |
| max-num-seqs 48 | J per on-time 1467 vs 836, on-time 27 vs 42 | fewer slots -> longer queue -> more requests miss the TTFT SLO, which costs more than the TPOT gained | no, keep 64 |
| Prefill chunk 2048 tokens (vs 8192) | grader val 48.7 -> 32.6: p95 TPOT 51 -> 50 ms, but J/request 213 -> 254 | smaller chunks stop long prompts from stalling decode, but each prompt now needs 4x more forward passes, and each pass re-reads the weights; energy (60% weight) lost more than latency (20%) gained | no, 8192 |
| SLO-aware scheduler (feasible earliest-deadline-first, missed requests oldest-first) | local Final proxy: on time 138-143 vs 165 for newest-first; p95 TTFT/SLO 135-168 vs 225 | aimed to keep newest-first's on-time gains while shrinking the TTFT tail. Under this overload far more than 5% of requests sit deep in the backlog in any order, so p95 stays huge; and EDF spends slots on requests that barely make their deadline, so fewer finish on time. Newest-first is the better trade for this score | no, newest-first |
| Shortest-prompt-first priority (INT8, after the backlog was gone) | local Final proxy: 212 on time vs 241-248 for newest-first, though the best TTFT tail (17x SLO vs 132-162x) | short prompts have the tightest TTFT deadline, but RAG and long-context prompts are a large share of the traffic; pushed to the back during the peak, they miss their own (longer) deadlines | no, newest-first |
| 80 concurrent sequences (INT8) | grader Final 99.6 vs 99.7 at 72; sT 0.98 both, 160 vs 152 J/request | past 72 the extra slots no longer add on-time peak requests, only a little energy | no, 72 |
| Thinking budget 512 / 256 (AWQ) | local: 512 R 0.976 (math 0.91); 256 produced *more* output tokens than 512 and p95 TPOT 54 ms | a hard cap that low cuts needed reasoning; at 256 the model continues reasoning inside the visible answer instead | no, 1024 then 2048 |
| Thinking budget 1024 on the grader (AWQ) | Final 48.6 but R 0.955 (A 0.90) | shorter reasoning costs math/long-context accuracy; 1536-2048 keeps A at 1.00 | replaced by 2048 |
| High concurrency (128-192 seqs), no MTP, small prefill chunks (2048-4096) + long-prefill threshold | local Final proxy: 34-42 on time (vs 146-171), p95 TPOT 118-189 ms, mJ/token 228-267 | a pure-decode benchmark (short prompts, 512 tokens) held TPOT at 27 ms for 128 seqs, but real requests carry 2-25K-token contexts plus ~1K thinking tokens; at 128+ sequences the attention reads over those contexts and the constant stream of prefills push every step past 100 ms. Lesson: benchmark capacity with realistic context lengths | no, 40 seqs |
| Power cap 350 W (mild) | local cold-cache: 249.5 vs 256-261 mJ/token at 400 W; grader Final: 40.0 -> 32.8, 179 J/req both, SLO met 169 -> 148, TPOT 44 -> 47 ms | the GPU averages ~336 W at 400 W, so 350 W only clips peaks — but those peaks are the busiest decode steps; clipping them slowed the peak phase just enough to push requests past the SLO, with no net energy saving on real traffic. Confirms the 250 W lesson at a smaller scale | no, 400 W |
| MTP off under overload (lecture: "speculation hurts at high batch") | local: 188 vs 155-166 on time, 452 vs 536-573 J per on-time; grader Final: 36.3 vs 40.0 — 176 vs 169 on time but 208 vs 179 J/request | partly right: without drafts more requests met the SLO (sT .45 vs .41). But per output token MTP is still ~3% cheaper (256-261 vs 268 mJ), and the local "energy win" came from that run happening to generate 8% fewer tokens. Lesson: compare energy per output token, and repeat runs, before trusting a local energy gain | no, keep MTP |
| Prefill chunk 16384 tokens (vs 8192) | local: 498 vs 536-573 J per on-time; grader Final: 40.0 -> 29.0, 179 -> 214 J/request, R 0.962 | the local gain was within run-to-run noise (two identical local runs differed by 11 on-time requests); on the grader, bigger chunks made long prefills block decoding for longer, and accuracy noise pushed R under 0.98. Lesson: don't trust a local gain smaller than the noise | no, 8192 |
| FP8 KV cache (`--kv-cache-dtype fp8`) | server failed to start | vLLM JIT-compiles an FP8 KV kernel at start-up and the VM has no CUDA toolkit (nvcc); installing one was not worth the time for an uncertain gain on a model where only 8 of 32 layers keep a KV cache | not tested |
| max-num-seqs 128 | KV 99% full, p95 TPOT 82-90 ms | too many sequences decoding at once: every request gets slower and most miss the 50 ms TPOT SLO | no, 64 |
